# Corrections to make on getbueno.com now

These are live articles. Each item below is wrong against a primary source, and the source
is named so a writer can fix it without re-doing the research. Ordered by how much damage
the error does.

## 1. squatter-laws: the 48-hour rule does not exist

**The article says:** squatters can be evicted immediately if discovered within 48 hours.

**The truth:** there is no 48-hour rule anywhere in Spanish law, and it is not Spanish
police practice either. The origin of the myth is visible in the source document itself.
Instruccion 1/2020 of the Fiscalia General del Estado mentions 48 hours only as a
description of **French** police powers, alongside a 24-hour reference for Germany. Spain
has no equivalent.

What Spain has instead is a test, not a clock: solid indications the offence was committed,
urgency and harm to the legitimate possessor, and proportionality.

**Why it matters most:** this is the top-read article on the blog, and an owner who
believes the rule will act on a deadline that does not exist, and may conclude at hour 50
that nothing can be done. The correct framing is that speed matters because it supports
flagrancia and the urgency test, not because a statute grants a window.

Source: [Instruccion 1/2020 FGE](https://www.boe.es/diario_boe/txt.php?id=BOE-A-2020-11243)

**Add while you are in there, because it helps the reader and the article omits it:** a
second home in regular personal use can be a *morada*, which makes the occupation the more
serious offence under article 202 rather than the fine-level offence under 245.2. And the
article's "18 months to 2 years" is out of date: Ley Organica 1/2025 put both offences on
the fast-track criminal route from 3 April 2025, with trial listed within fifteen days of
the guard court stage.

## 2. cost-of-buying-expats: the non-resident tax base is wrong

**The article says:** "the taxable amount is usually 1.1% of the entire value of the
property."

**The truth:** the base is a percentage of the **cadastral** value from the IBI receipt,
not market or purchase value. And the rate is 1.1 percent only where the cadastral value
was revised in the relevant period; otherwise it is 2 percent.

**Why it matters:** as written this overstates the tax several times over, because cadastral
values sit well below market. This is the single most-repeated Bueno tax figure and it is
wrong in two independent ways.

Source: [AEAT, calculo de la renta imputada](https://sede.agenciatributaria.gob.es/Sede/vivienda-otros-inmuebles/imputacion-rentas-inmobiliarias/calculo-renta-imputada.html)

## 3. Rental filing is no longer quarterly, and one deadline is a month away

**The articles say:** quarterly declarations on 20 April, July, October and January.

**The truth:** the grouping period moved from quarterly to annual for accruals from 2024,
and Orden HAC/623/2026 then moved the window to April. The last quarterly rental filing is
**Q3 2026, due 1 to 20 October 2026**. Income accrued from 1 October 2026 goes into an
annual return filed 1 to 20 April 2027.

Separately, for imputed income the direct debit deadline is **23 December**, eight days
before the filing deadline of 31 December. That gap is the most common way an owner
believes they have paid and has not. No article mentions it.

**Why it matters:** this is worth an email to customers this month, not just an article fix.

Sources: [Orden HAC/623/2026](https://www.boe.es/buscar/doc.php?id=BOE-A-2026-13573),
[AEAT note on the deadline changes](https://sede.agenciatributaria.gob.es/Sede/todas-gestiones/impuestos-tasas/impuesto-sobre-renta-no-residentes/modelo-210-irnr______a-no-residentes-permanente_/nota-modificaciones-plazos-presentacion-modelo-210.html)

## 4. The national rental registry was struck down

**The articles say:** you need an NRA registration number, in force from 1 July 2025, with
an annual filing window of 1 February to 2 March.

**The truth:** the Tribunal Supremo annulled the registration procedure in three judgments
in May and June 2026, on the ground that the State lacks competence to create the registry.
The registration obligation, the assignment of the number and the annual information
machinery are all void. The Ventanilla Unica and the platform data-sharing duties survive.

The annual window as described never existed anyway. What existed was an informative
declaration filed during the month of February, not a renewal of the number.

**What still binds an owner is the REGIONAL licence**, which is unaffected and varies by
region and increasingly by municipality. Platforms may still be asking for a number.

Source: [STS 19 May 2026](https://www.boe.es/diario_boe/txt.php?id=BOE-A-2026-12300),
plus judgments of 21 May and 1 June 2026.

## 5. supreme-court-tourist-ban: the majority now works the other way

**The article says:** three fifths of owners can ban tourist lets.

**The truth:** since 3 April 2025 the same three-fifths majority is what is needed to
**approve** a tourist let in the first place. The word "apruebe" was added to article 17.12
by Ley Organica 1/2025. It is a majority of owners **and** of participation quotas.

Existing lets are grandfathered only where the tourist licence or declaracion responsable
was obtained before 3 April 2025. A prior municipal urbanistic compatibility report is not
enough.

Sources: [LPH consolidated](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1960-10906),
[DGSJFP resolution](https://www.boe.es/diario_boe/txt.php?id=BOE-A-2026-1528)

## 6. nota-simple: banks do not lend against the cadastral value

**The article says:** the bank looks at the cadastral value to decide the mortgage.

**The truth:** banks lend against the **tasacion**, the appraisal. Cadastral values sit
well below market, so as written the article tells buyers they cannot borrow when they can.

The article also spells "catastral" as "castral" throughout.

## 7. estate-planning: two wrong numbers and a conflated concept

- **The state ISD scale tops out at 34 percent, not 36.5.** Source:
  [art. 21 Ley 29/1987](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1987-28141).
  Worth adding: the article 22 multipliers by kinship group run up to 2.4, so an unmarried
  partner faces an effective rate well above 34.
- **The late-filing surcharge schedule is the pre-2021 regime.** It is now 1 percent plus
  1 percent per complete month to twelve months, then 15 percent plus interest. Ley 11/2021
  replaced the old tiers.
- **The legitima description conflates two different things.** "50 percent of the joint
  property goes to the spouse" is the matrimonial property regime, which decides what is in
  the estate. The thirds are the forced heirship rules, which decide how the estate is
  divided. Run as one calculation it overstates the children's entitlement by roughly half.

**Add the fact the article is missing and that readers most need:** under EU Regulation
650/2012 a Norwegian, Swedish, German, British or French owner can elect their own national
law and displace the Spanish legitima entirely, but only through a will that says so.

## 8. Golden visa: three articles, three dates

brexit-impact says April 2024, key-questions says April 2025, noneu-100-tax says 3 April
2025. The last is right. Ley Organica 1/2025 closed the whole investor route, not only the
property variant, on **3 April 2025**. brexit-impact also gives the threshold in dollars;
the scheme was denominated in euros.

## 9. The 100 percent tax on non-EU buyers is a proposal, not law

key-questions and scams both refer to it as though it may be in force. No enacting
instrument exists, and the Ministry of Hacienda's 2026 comparative publication records no
non-resident or foreign-buyer surcharge anywhere in Spain. Say "proposed" plainly.

## 10. buying-fees: the regional tax table

Andalucia is shown at 8 percent. It has been 7 percent since Ley 5/2021. Several other
rows are stale, and the table flattens banded regimes into single rates, which is wrong for
Balears, Catalunya, Aragon, Castilla y Leon and Extremadura. The verified table is in
`rules/itp-ajd.json`.

Also: the Canary Islands row is labelled "VAT 7 percent". The Canaries charge **IGIC**, not
VAT. The rate is right and the label is wrong, and a reader searching for Canary VAT finds
nothing.

## 11. nie-application: wrong form number

The article says EX-18. The NIE application is **EX-15**. EX-18 is the EU citizen
registration certificate, a different procedure. The current fee is **9.84 euros**
(Orden PJC/617/2025).

Also, two articles say the NIE is valid for three months. The **number** is permanent; the
**certificate** is what has a three-month life. Safe wording: "Your NIE number stays with
you. The certificate that proves it is only accepted for three months, so banks, notaries
and tax offices may ask for a fresh one."

## 12. legally-renting: the fine figures are not supported

"4,000 to 60,000 euros, up to 111,000 in Ibiza" could not be traced to any regional law and
is wrong for the Balearics, where article 120 of Ley 8/2012 sets very serious infractions at
**40,001 to 400,000 euros**. There is no 111,000 ceiling. Figures for the Comunitat
Valenciana and Andalucia could not be verified at all, so publish none for those two.

## 13. Smaller conflicts to reconcile

The reservation fee is given as four different ranges across four articles. The land
registry fee is 400 to 700 in one and 500 to 1,000 in another. The NIE fee is 9.84 in one
and 12 in another. Notary fees are given as "0.01% to 2%", a range spanning a factor of two
hundred, where a state tariff gives a real scale. The arras penalty is described once as
"20% back" and once as "double", which are the same outcome expressed two ways that a
developer will code differently. IBI is 0.3 to 1.1 percent of cadastral value in one
article and 0.4 to 1.1 percent of property value in another, and one of those bases is
wrong by a factor of two or three.

Pick one figure for each, correct the others, and the calculators will match whichever
article the reader arrived from.
