The Complete Guide to Getting Citizenship in Spain
Becoming a Spanish citizen is a big milestone. Whether Spain has become your long-term home, you’ve built a life there with family, or you’re planning your future around Spanish property and investment, citizenship offers stability, freedom and full access to life in Spain and the EU.


This guide is designed to help you understand what becoming a Spanish citizen really involves, from eligibility and the application process to what changes once you finally hold Spanish nationality.


Who Can Apply for Spanish Citizenship?
Spain offers several legal routes to citizenship, each with different requirements depending on your background, nationality and length of time in the country.


Citizenship by Residency
Living in the country is by far the most common way to become a Spanish citizen. To qualify, you must have lived in Spain legally and continuously for a minimum period immediately before applying. In most cases, this period is 10 years, but Spain may reduce the requirement in certain circumstances.


Continuity matters. Long absences from Spain can reset the clock, even if you hold a valid permit.


After Brexit, many Brits decided to get Spanish citizenship to make life in the country easier. It allowed them to continue their lives normally, without the need to apply for different permits.


Citizenship by Marriage
Marriage to a Spanish citizen does not grant automatic citizenship. However, if you have been married and living together in Spain for at least one year with legal residency, you may apply. The authorities will look closely at the authenticity of the marriage and shared life in Spain.


Citizenship by Descent
If you have Spanish parents, or in some cases Spanish grandparents, you may qualify for citizenship by origin. Spain also has specific provisions for descendants of Spaniards who went into exile. These cases are highly individual and often require unique documentation (with dates) and specialist advice and guidance.


Citizenship by Birth
Some babies born in Spain may automatically become citizens if their parents cannot pass on their own nationality or if certain specific legal conditions apply.


Being born in Spain, however, does not automatically make a child Spanish in the general way that countries with full birthright citizenship do. Spain primarily uses citizenship by descent, meaning nationality is passed through parents.


How to Apply for Spanish Citizenship Step by Step
For the purpose of this blog, we’ll presume you’ve bought property in Spain and have been living there for more than 10 years (or qualify for a reduced period).
Step 1. Confirm You Meet The Legal Requirements
Before starting, ensure you meet the required residency period and that your stay in Spain has been legal and continuous. If it’s not, you won’t be able to apply.


Even if you have permanent residency, spending long periods abroad can delay or invalidate an application.


Step 2. Prepare for The Exams
Most applicants must pass two exams to gain Spanish citizenship. The Instituto Cervantes administers these exams. The first is the CCSE, which tests knowledge of Spanish society, culture, laws and government. The second is the DELE A2, which demonstrates a basic level of Spanish language ability.


Some applicants are exempt from the language exam, particularly those from Spanish-speaking countries or those with a Spanish education.


Exam places fill up very quickly in big cities, so you should book early.


Step 3. Gather and Legalise All The Necessary Documents
Gathering all the necessary documentation is one of the most time-consuming aspects of the process. You will need official certificates proving your identity, residency and good conduct. You’ll probably need your birth certificate, criminal record certificates (from Spain and any other country you’ve lived in), proof of legal residence and your certificado de empadronamiento.


Foreign documents must usually be legalised or apostilled and translated by a sworn translator in Spain.


Step 4. Submit The Application
While it’s possible to apply on your own, it’s best to do it under legal guidance to ensure accuracy. Errors, missing documents or inconsistencies can add months to the processing time.


Step 5. Monitor The Application
Processing times vary widely. What may take a couple of months on the Costa Blanca could take years in a larger city (or the other way around)! During this period, the Ministry may request additional documents or clarifications. Responding quickly is essential.


Step 6. Complete The Oath
Once your application is approved, you must attend a jura ceremony at the Civil Registry. You have to swear allegiance to the Spanish Constitution and King and formally renounce your previous nationality if required (if you don’t have dual nationality rights with Spain). After this step, you are legally Spanish!


Step 7. Apply for Your DNI and Passport
Once you’re a Spanish citizen, you can apply for your DNI (change it from a NIE to a DNI) and a Spanish passport.


Benefits of Becoming a Spanish Citizen
Spanish citizenship offers practical, legal, and lifestyle benefits. As a citizen, you can live and work anywhere in the European Union without visas or permits. You gain full political rights in Spain, including the ability to vote in national elections and stand for public office.


Being a citizen also means you don’t have to renew your residency permits, and it eliminates the risk of losing your status due to time spent abroad.


For families, citizenship simplifies inheritance and long-term planning, particularly when children are involved.


Spanish Citizenship vs Permanent Residency
Permanent residency is an important goal for those moving to Spain because it offers stability. It’s not, however, the same as citizenship. Permanent residents may live and work in Spain indefinitely, but their overall rights are more limited. If you leave Spain for a long time, you can lose your status, and those with permanent residency don’t benefit from EU-wide freedom of movement.


Permanent residents can’t vote in national elections and some public sector jobs are restricted to Spanish citizens. Citizenship, however, is usually lifelong and provides the strongest legal status available in Spain.


For those buying property, investing or planning retirement in Spain, citizenship offers an additional layer of certainty and flexibility.


Common Mistakes to Avoid When Applying for Spanish Citizenship
Several errors come up time and again when it comes to applying for Spanish citizenship. Make sure you avoid them to ensure your application has the best chance of being accepted.


* Applying before meeting the full residency requirements. This is particularly true where absences from Spain are involved. Make sure you’ve lived and paid taxes in the country for the required time.
* Submitting expired documents. Make sure all documents are valid. Some documents may only be valid for 90 days, so always check and get originals before submitting your application.
* Don’t underestimate the importance of the exams. The Spanish level needed isn’t extremely high, but it’s best to do practice papers or even a course to be prepared.
* Not responding to requests quickly. The quicker you respond, the quicker the issue gets resolved. Delaying requests about documents or your application can add months to the process.
Ensure you prepare everything well in advance (but so that documents are still valid!) to ensure your application is processed as quickly as possible.


Final Thoughts on Getting Citizenship in Spain
Spanish citizenship is not quick, but it is achievable with the right planning and realistic expectations. Understanding your eligibility, preparing documents early and staying organised throughout the process make a significant difference.
