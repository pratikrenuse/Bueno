Common Scams Targeting Foreign Property Owners in Spain and How to Avoid Them


Buying property in Spain is a dream for many. From sun-soaked coastlines to vibrant cities and relaxed lifestyles, it’s easy to see why so many international buyers decide to invest in property in the country. On the whole, Spain is a safe and well-regulated country, but like anywhere, scams do exist. Foreign property owners and buyers are often targeted simply because the system is unfamiliar and the language can be a barrier.


The good news is that most property-related scams in Spain are avoidable. Knowing what to look out for and setting yourself up with the right financial support can save you time, money and stress.

So what are the key scams to look out for and to avoid at all costs? Here’s our reliable guide to buying property in Spain securely.


Why Are Scams Such a Problem for Foreign Property Buyers?
Scammers love confusion and urgency. Many foreign buyers don’t fully understand Spanish property law, tax requirements, or banking practices, as these differ significantly from those in their home country.


Many rely heavily on third parties such as agents, lawyers or translators and, the more people involved, the more chance there is for a scammer to act. Scammers can also pretend that certain scams are normal in Spain and just ‘how things are done here’. A confused foreigner trying to get to grips with the process can easily fall for that.


Owning property from abroad means that most of the communication happens remotely. That creates opportunities for impersonation, misinformation and payment-related fraud.


Top 6 Property Scams Targeting Foreign Owners in Spain
#1. False or Misleading Property Listings
Sometimes, things are too good to be true. If you see a really good property in a popular location for a very attractive price, the likelihood is that it’s a scam. It’s one of the most common scams that involves properties being listed on popular sites that either don’t exist or don’t look anything like they do in the posted images.
Scammers might take real listings and post them somewhere else or seriously reduce the price to make them really attractive. In some cases, they’ll then ask for a reservation fee or deposit to secure the property before you’ve even seen it. They may even tell you that’s normal procedure in Spain. It’s not.


There are a couple of things you should look out for:


* Prices that seem too good to be true (significantly lower than similar properties in the area)
* Pressure to transfer money before viewing (often with a time limit)
* Sellers are unwilling to meet in person or are coming up with excuses why they can’t send the correct documentation


To avoid becoming a victim of a scam like this, always view a property in person or through a trusted representative. Of course, there are lots of benefits to virtual property viewings too, but someone should always view them in person.


It’s also essential to work with established agents and never transfer funds without legal confirmation.


#2. Bait and Switch Property Scams
Bait and switch scams are particularly common in cities and popular coastal towns such as those on the Costa Blanca. A buyer enquires about a property that looks perfect – the price, style and location are ideal, and the listing looks legit.


Once the buyer makes contact with the seller to arrange a viewing, they’re then told that the property has just been sold or is no longer available. But now they’ve hooked you in. They offer you a similar property and, because you trust the quality of the listing, you’re happy to have a look. The only catch is that the new property is more expensive and doesn’t meet the original requirements.


This practice isn’t always illegal but it is misleading. It can also put a lot of pressure on foreign buyers, especially if they’ve travelled to Spain and are emotionally committed to buying.


Things you can do to avoid this scam are:


* Check how long the property has been on the market
* Be wary of listings that are posted more often than others
* Set a clear budget and don’t feel pressured to increase it


#3. Impersonation Scams (Lawyers and Agents)
This is one of the more sophisticated scams happening in Spain. It involves criminals pretending to be estate agents, lawyers and even notaries. They can intercept emails and alter payment instructions, which makes fake bank details appear legitimate.


These scams often rely on urgency, confusion and last-minute changes. They may say something like ‘If you don’t pay by the end of today, the property will be sold to another buyer’.


In order to protect yourself from such scams:


* Always verify bank details verbally or in person before sending money. Agents often give you a formal document with the bank details clearly outlined.
* Be very cautious of any changes to payment instructions. If you are asked to send money to a different location, double-check (preferably in person) with your agent.
* Use secure and trusted communication channels. Foreigners are often targeted because they live abroad and rely on emails. It might be worth getting someone you trust or a power of attorney to do in-person meetings for you.


#4. Illegal Holiday Rentals
Many people buy property in Spain with the idea of renting it out for some extra income. But because of the property crisis that’s currently affecting the country, the government has implemented several measures to reduce holiday rentals and foreign property owners, such as the proposed 100% property tax for non-EU buyers. Another measure is to reduce the number of properties eligible for a tourist license.


If your property does not have a valid tourist license or is in an area where it’s extremely difficult to obtain one, you cannot legally rent it for short-term or holiday rentals.


These rental regulations offer the perfect scenario for scammers. Buyers are often told the property has a tourist license or is in an area where one is obtainable (and neither is true). Buying a property that cannot legally be rented can result in fines and loss of expected income.


Always check:


* Whether short-term rentals are permitted in that area
* If a license does exist, that it can be transferred between owners
* Any community or regional restrictions (that might come into play in the future)


#5. Community Fees and Tax-Related Scams
There are lots of added fees when it comes to buying property in Spain. Community fees, non-resident income tax, and property tax can get pretty confusing. Again, this confusion is ideal for scammers.


Foreign property owners might receive fake or higher payment demands, including claims of unpaid taxes, community fees or maintenance costs. These messages are often urgent and can be threatening.


It’s important not to panic and to not pay the fee or fine without checking first. Always speak to your community administrator, relevant tax authority or legal advisor before. You can also check the Bueno blog, as there are several articles on fees and taxes you can expect to pay when buying property in Spain.


#6. Banking Pitfalls
OK, maybe this isn’t exactly a scam, but hidden bank fees can cost foreign property owners far more than expected.


Many banks in Spain advertise free banking, but this often comes with conditions that non-residents struggle to meet. Monthly fees, transaction charges and bad exchange rates can quickly add up, especially when managing property expenses from abroad.
