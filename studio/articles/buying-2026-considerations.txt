Key Considerations for Buying Property in Spain in 2026
Spain has long been one of Europe’s most desirable countries to buy property – and it doesn’t look like that’s going to change in 2026. International interest is steady, lifestyle-driven relocation continues to grow and lots of buyers see Spanish property as an excellent long term investment.


But there are a few things that have changed when it comes to buying property in Spain in 2026. The regulatory and financial landscape around buying and renting, particularly for non-residents, has become a bit more complicated over the last few years.


If you’re considering a purchase this year, understanding what’s new, expected trends and what matters most will help you find the perfect property and avoid surprises later.


Is 2026 Still a Good Year to Invest in Spanish Property?
For many foreign property buyers, Spain still ticks all the boxes. The lifestyle is great, there’s long term value and the rental opportunities are consistent, even in lower seasons. This combination continues to cause property prices to grow across the county, even if it’s more moderate compared to earlier years.


As always, where you buy property in Spain is key. New rules have been put in place which restrict short term rentals, but if your property allows or already has a tourist license, you can make a great side income from renting it out to remote workers, retirees and holiday makers in the warmer months. If it suits your situation, you can also rent out mid or long term for consistency.


Compared to many other European countries, Spain still offers much more for your money. One of the biggest draws for investing in property in 2026 is comparing what you could buy in your home country compared to what’s on offer in Spain. That, and views of the Mediterranean and the promise of sun, of course!


Key Things to Know About When Buying Property in Spain in 2026
#1. Rental Registration and Registration Requirements
Not so long ago, if you had property in Spain, you could easily rent it out to holiday makers for an extra income. But in recent years, the Spanish government has implemented several measures in a bid to crack down on illegal short-term rentals which are driving up the prices for locals.


Regulation of rental activity is one of the biggest areas of change affecting foreign owners in 2026. Only a few years ago, it was much easier to rent out property as there were hardly any restrictions as to who could get a tourist license. Nowadays, in popular rental areas, it’s almost impossible to get a tourist license which means property owners need to look towards mid and long term rentals.


If you plan to rent out your property – even occasionally – you’ll probably need to register for an NRA (Número de Registro de Alquiler) before listing the property on popular websites such as Airbnb and Booking.com. Even if you have a tourist license and rent the property out short term, you’ll need to have this number to list on online platforms.


This NRA number will need to be renewed every year for you to continue renting out your property legally. This usually needs to be done by March, but you can check out our month by month checklist for non-resident property owners in Spain for more guidance.


The key thing when it comes to renting in Spain is that it’s no longer just about demand and seasons – it’s about compliance. Rules vary by region and property, so it’s essential to confirm what’s permitted before you buy.


#2. Financing and Mortgages for Foreign Buyers in Spain in 2026
Financing property purchases in Spain is very popular. Over the years, Spanish banks have been happy to lend to non-residents, although they do do several financial and affordability tests.


Typical things to consider when getting a mortgage in 2026 include:


* Paying a deposit of 30 - 40%
* Detailed financial checks
* Preference for fixed-rate mortgage products
* Closer assessment where rental income supports affordability


In 2026, it’s a good idea to contact Spanish banks well in advance to get an idea of how much they’ll be willing to lend you for your estimated budget. Knowing your financial situation in advance can help to speed up the property buying process, so you don’t miss out on your dream home.


But a word of warning – free banking in Spain is very rarely free, so it’s essential to check the conditions carefully with each bank.


#3. Property Taxation
Understanding tax obligations in Spain continues to be one of the most overlooked yet important parts of buying property in the country. They often differ between municipalities and it’s important to be fully informed to avoid potential penalties and late payments.


In 2026, foreign property buyers should pay particular attention to:


* Ongoing tax obligations for non-resident owners (such as Non-Resident Property Tax)
* Regional differences in wealth tax exposure
* Purchase-related taxes that vary by property type and location


Small structural decisions made before purchase, as well as speaking to a tax expert in Spain, can have a long-term impact on overall returns.




#4. Energy Performance and Property Standards
Energy efficiency is becoming increasingly important across Spain, which means it’s becoming a more visible factor in property transactions. Energy performance certificates are not only a legal requirement but also add value to the property.


Modern properties already comply with the fairly strict regulations in place. But older properties may need extra investment, especially if insulation is poor or the systems are inefficient. If you decide to buy an older building in Spain, it’s important to think about the future compliance regulations you’ll need to comply with as well as long term attractiveness and rentability.


#5. Choosing The Right Location in 2026
The Spanish property market is very diverse and regional, which means conditions can differ significantly between locations. A huge factor as to where is best for you to buy property in Spain in 2026 depends on whether you want to rent it out or not.


Broad trends for 2026 include:


* Demand for major cities which are popular with digital nomads
* Established coastal areas, such as the Costa Blanca, where owners can enjoy Spain’s sunny beaches and there’s excellent renting potential
* Good deals in emerging markets, such as lesser-known neighbourhoods or smaller coastal towns
* Looking inland towards small, rural villages


Your decision should reflect your primary goal, whether that’s rental income, lifestyle use or capital growth. Choosing the right location remains one of the most important factors in long-term investment success.


Buying Property in Spain in 2026
The good news is that 2026 is still an excellent year to invest in property in Spain. The market may have slowed down slightly over the last few years, but buyers can still find excellently priced properties with long-term rental potential and value.


The key considerations for 2026 are non-resident tax obligations, property restrictions and registration for rentals. It’s important to keep up to date with local changes so you don’t miss anything new and face fines or penalties in the future.
