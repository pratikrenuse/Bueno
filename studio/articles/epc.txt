The Importance of Energy Performance Certificates (EPCs) in Spain


Energy Performance Certificates (EPCs) are a crucial document for everyone selling and buying property in Spain. They play an important role in promoting energy efficiency and sustainability in Spain’s property market. Not only are they important in terms of the environment, but they also help buyers, tenants and owners to understand a property’s energy consumption and how it could improve.


What Is an Energy Performance Certificate (EPC) in Spain?
An EPC is an official document that rates the energy efficiency of a building, providing crucial information about its energy consumption and environmental impact to prospective buyers and tenants. It shows how energy efficient a building is and suggests practical improvements that can reduce energy use (and waste) and the associated costs.


The EPC uses a colour-coded scale from A to G, which is easy to understand. An A result represents the most energy-efficient buildings with the lowest energy consumption. G, however, indicates the least efficient score.


The certificate takes several important factors into account, such as heating and cooling systems, the property’s insulation, lighting and general energy demand. It also contains some recommendations for improving the property’s energy efficiency.


How Long Is an EPC Valid in Spain?
EPCs are valid for 10 years in Spain. After that, you’ll need to get another one issued before selling the property. If you sell the property within those 10 years, you don’t have to have a new one issued, although you might want to – especially if you’ve done updates to the property, which could improve its score.


Why Are Energy Performance Certificates Important in Spain?
There are several reasons why EPCs are so important when selling and buying property in Spain. Not only are they a legal requirement, but they also help to keep everyone’s focus on green improvements for the future.


Importance for Property Owners
To avoid fines, property owners must have a valid EPC when selling or renting their property. Not only does it ensure they’re legally compliant, but it also has the potential to increase the property value (especially if it has a good score).


Furthermore, a valid EPC gives property owners access to green funding and subsidies which they otherwise wouldn’t be able to benefit from.
Importance for Buyers and Tenants
For buyers and tenants, EPCs are essential because they provide transparent information on long-term energy costs. Buyers can then use them to make an informed decision based on the EPC's financial implications.
Importance for Real Estate Professionals
EPCs are not just crucial for those buying and selling property, but they also have benefits for those in the industry. Energy certificates help to standardise everything, meaning estate agents can easily find out if a property is energy efficient and therefore worth more. These certificates help to make properties more attractive and build trust with eco-conscious clients.
How to Obtain an Energy Performance Certificate in Spain
Luckily, getting an EPC in Spain is relatively easy. The first thing you need to do is hire a certified technician or a técnico certificador. They will then carry out an on-site inspection, considering factors such as insulation, windows, heating and cooling systems and lighting.


Once that’s completed, they’ll issue the certificate with their findings and rating. The certificate also needs to be registered with the appropriate regional authority – which the technician will probably do.


How Much Does an EPC in Spain Cost?
The price of an EPC can vary greatly and usually depends on where the property is located, its size and the technicians available. Usually, however, it’s between €100 and €300. From start to finish, it should take less than a week to receive your EPC.


Is It Compulsory to Have an Energy Performance Certificate in Spain?
Yes, it is compulsory to present an EPC if you’re thinking of selling property in Spain, which includes primary residences and holiday homes. EPCs are also required for non-residential buildings such as businesses, hospitals and schools.


Can You Be Fined for Not Having an Energy Performance Certificate?
Yes, failing to supply an EPC when you sell your property can result in a pretty substantial fine. Usually, fines range between €300 and €6,000 depending on the severity of the offence. For example, if you advertise your property without a valid EPC, then you can expect to pay between €300 and €600.


However, if you fail to provide an EPC altogether or falsify the information in it, you can expect a much higher fine of €1,000 to €6,000. Fines and regulations are managed by autonomous regions, and they all have their own way of dealing with inspections and penalties.


Energy Performance Certificates: Final Thoughts
An EPC is an essential document for anyone wanting to sell or buy property in Spain. Not providing one can result in a large fine. EPCs are helping Spain reach their national and EU climate goals and push towards net-zero buildings. They also help with transparency when it comes to selling or renting property, so everyone is aware of the property’s energy consumption and the associated fees.


The good news is that when you have a fully functioning Spanish bank account, it’s easy to apply for an EPC. They’re an important part of the selling process for owners, buyers and property professionals and should not be overlooked.
