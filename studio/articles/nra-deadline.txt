Don’t Miss The NRA Deadline: What Property Owners in Spain Need to Know


If you rent out property in Spain for short-term, mid-term or tourist stays, then you need to make sure you’re compliant with the new NRA requirements. Failure to do so will mean you’re unable to legally post your rental property on major booking platforms, and therefore may miss out on rental income.


As well as obtaining an NRA number, property owners need to renew their registration and submit an annual report to remain compliant.


But what details need to be in this report? How can you complete the process? And when’s the deadline? Here’s everything you need to know.


What Is The NRA, and Who Needs One?
The NRA (Número de Registro de Alquiler) is a compulsory registration number for all properties that offer short-term, mid-term and tourist rentals. You have to put this number on all rental platforms to ensure you’re legally complying with Spanish law. It confirms that the property is officially registered for rental activity and authorised to be advertised.


Why Do You Need an NRA?
This number is one of the Spanish government’s initiatives to reduce the number of illegal tourist rentals, which are significantly driving up prices for those looking for longer stays. It’s a way to safely and legally advertise your property online, and to ensure all taxes and licenses are in order.


The Annual NRA Renewal and Filing Requirement
The NRA came into force on 1st July 2025 but it’s not just a one-time formality. Property owners looking to continue renting must renew and update their registration each year.


The annual process serves two purposes:
* Confirming the property is still actively used for rentals
* Updating official records with current activity information


You need to do this even if you only rented out your property once in the year.
When Does The Filing Need to Be Submitted?
For rentals in 2025, the filing window is between 1st February and 2nd March 2026.


What You Need to Report to Renew Your NRA in Spain
Transparency is the most important thing when filing your report. While requirements can vary slightly by region, property owners are generally expected to provide:


* Confirmation that the property is still being used for rental use
* Any changes to ownership
* Rental activity during the year (different rentals, length of time, etc.)
* Reasons for rental stay (studying, working abroad, holiday, etc.)
* Confirmation that safety and habitability standards are maintained


If there haven’t been any changes to your property or activity, you still need to submit a report to ensure the details remain accurate.


It’s a good idea to keep a record of all the rentals throughout the year, as it will make the process much easier.


How to Complete The NRA Annual Filing
Those already with an NRA should get a reminder email in January about the renewal process. Property owners have two main options: submitting the renewal themselves or working with a professional to handle the process for you.


Option 1: Doing The Renewal Yourself
You can complete the filing through the Spanish land registry (Registro de la Propiedad). Make sure you contact the one that your property is registered under. This is an easy and cheaper option if you speak Spanish and feel confident handling bureaucratic tasks in the language.


The process usually involves:
* Accessing the official registration platform
* Confirming property identification details
* Updating any changes in ownership or rental activity (listing the reason for stay, too)
* Submitting the required declaration before the deadline


The website for the land registry isn’t always straightforward and you may run into some technical difficulties. For instance, you can only download the relevant forms from the land registry in Valencia to a Microsoft computer, not an Apple. In this case, you might need to visit them in person. Be sure to check the operating times as they’re not always regular.


Option 2: Use a Professional Service
Many property owners in Spain choose to hire a gestor, lawyer or compliance specialist who can handle these filings on their behalf. Some smaller rental platforms may even offer this service for an extra fee.


A professional service can:
* Ensure your information is submitted correctly
* Guide you through the process
* Monitor deadlines
* Handle communication with authorities (including follow-up questions)
* Reduce the risk of errors


Why Does NRA Compliance Matter?
For those who have rented out property in Spain for years, complying with the new NRA rules can feel like an extra hassle. But it’s not just a regulatory formality – it directly affects your ability to rent legally and protect your investment.


Renewing your NRA allows you to keep renting out your property legally and ensures you avoid fines and financial penalties. Successful registration also ensures your rental activity is lawful, which is important for insurance coverage, dispute resolution and long-term investment security.


Failure to comply with the NRA laws has consequences, including:
* Temporary or permanent removal from rental platforms
* Administrative penalties or fines
* Increased scrutiny from regional authorities in the future




Don’t Miss The NRA Renewal Deadline
Spain’s NRA system is designed to create clarity and regulation in the short and mid-term rental market in a bid to tackle the housing crisis. The annual reporting and renewal of the NRA is a simple but essential requirement that allows you to legally carry on renting out your property.


Whether you choose to file independently or use a specialist, the key is to act before the deadline and keep a record throughout the year of all your rental activity.
