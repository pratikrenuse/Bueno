How to Change a NIE to a DNI in Spain
If you’ve recently become a Spanish citizen (enhorabuena!), one of the first bits of admin you’ll need to tackle is swapping your foreigner’s identification number (Número de Identificación de Extranjero) for a Spanish national identity document (DNI). The process is straightforward once you understand the steps, but it’s not always obvious where to start, and like many Spanish bureaucratic applications, it can feel overwhelming.


This guide will walk you through what changes (and what doesn’t), what documents you’ll need, and exactly how to make the switch.


A Quick Word on Getting Spanish Nationality
There are several paths to Spanish citizenship: by residency (usually 10 years, less in some cases), by marriage, by descent, or through specific national programmes. Once granted, you receive a resolución de concesión de nacionalidad and eventually complete the jura (oath) at the Civil Registry. After this, your NIE no longer applies. Spanish citizens use the DNI as their primary identification.


How Can You Change Your NIE to a DNI in Spain? Step-by-Step Guide
1. Book Your DNI Appointment
You must apply at the Comisaría de Policía authorised to issue DNIs (it may be the same place you got your NIE). Appointments can be made through the official website by selecting “DNI” as the procedure. Appointments in large cities tend to fill up quickly, so it's best to book well in advance. It’s also worth checking early in the morning or late in the evening for cancellations.


2. Gather The Documents You’ll Need
The police require original documents plus photocopies. This is why we always recommend getting extra copies of your certificado de empadronamiento and other important documents.


* Your Certificado Literal de Nacimiento. This is issued by the Civil Registry for DNI purposes. When you acquire Spanish nationality, Spain doesn’t treat it as a change of status on your existing foreign record. Instead, the state creates a Spanish Civil Registry birth record for you, even if you were born in another country.


The Civil Registry should automatically send your birth registration to the police system, but taking a printed copy avoids delays.
* Certificado de Empadronamiento. This needs to be a recent and original one, usually issued within the last 90 days.
* Your Previous Spanish Foreign ID. This could be your NIE or TIE card or passport from your country of origin if you don’t have one.
* Recent Passport Photo. Make sure that the photo meets Spain’s requirements. It needs to be 32mm high and 26mm wide. It also has to have a plain background, and the person must be front-facing with a neutral expression.
* Payment for the DNI Fee. You usually pay this in person at the appointment in cash or by card, depending on the office. It costs around €12.


3. Attend the Appointment
At the police station, an officer will review your documents, take fingerprints and confirm all the data you’re handing over. The process typically takes 10-15 minutes if everything is in order.


You’ll receive the DNI on the spot or a collection slip telling you when to return. Many stations now print it immediately.


4. Update Your Details
Once you have your DNI, it’s important to update it everywhere. Remember to update it with:


* Your Spanish Bank
* Local tax office and social security
* Healthcare provider
* Driving licence authority (DGT)
* Utility companies
* Your local ayuntamiento, if needed


This ensures all your records match and prevents delays and confusion in the future.


Changing a NIE to a DNI: Final Thoughts
Changing from a NIE to a DNI isn’t really a conversion; it’s the final administrative step after becoming a Spanish citizen. While the paperwork can feel daunting, understanding what’s required in advance helps to avoid delays and repeat appointments.


Taking the time to prepare everything properly means you can move smoothly from foreign resident to fully recognised Spanish national.
