What Happens After Your Offer Is Accepted on a Spanish Property?
Having your offer accepted on a Spanish property is an exciting time. Whether it’s a holiday home for you and your family, you’re planning a permanent move, or it’s a smart investment opportunity, it means you’ve found the perfect place and things are starting to feel real.


But unlike some other countries, where the process may feel familiar, buying property in Spain has its own pace, paperwork, and way of doing things.


So what actually happens after you’ve had an offer accepted?


Here’s everything you need to know about the practical realities, timelines and the key differences every foreign buyer should be aware of.


#1. The Property Comes Off the Market
Once you’ve had your offer formally accepted, the first thing that happens is that the property comes off all platforms, so no one else can make an offer. But, it’s important to understand that this stage is not yet legally binding (especially not in the way that some buyers might expect).


In Spain, you could call the acceptance of an offer a gentleman’s agreement. While it signals intent from both parties and often goes ahead without complications, either side can still walk away without formal penalties.


In Spain, the real commitment to buying a property comes later on.


#2. The Offer Contract
This is often part of taking the property off the market. You’ll need to sign an official document, usually at the estate agent’s if you’re using one, that legally states your offer.


You’ll then transfer the estate agent a sum (between €1,000 and €2,000) which can be taken off their fees if the sale goes ahead, or which will be returned to you if it falls through.


#3. The Arras Contract (Contrato de Arras)
One of the biggest steps when buying property in Spain is signing the Arras Contract. It can feel pretty significant to those not used to how the Spanish system works.


The Arras Contract is a kind of deposit contract, and signing this is when things become legally significant.


At this stage:
* You typically pay a deposit (usually around 10% of the purchase price)
* The terms of the sale are formally agreed upon
* A completion date is set


Transferring 10% of the purchase price to a secure bank (or, in some cases, although we wouldn’t recommend it, directly to the seller) can feel very strange for foreign property buyers. But what makes the Arras Contract particularly important is how it protects both sides.


If the buyer pulls out of the sale, they lose this deposit (yes, you lose 10% of the purchase price)! But, and it’s a big but, if the seller pulls out, they have to pay you double the amount back.


The great thing about the Arras Contract, which can’t be said about other countries such as the UK, is that both parties sign it with the intention of the sale going through. There’s no chance of messing around or parties unexpectedly pulling out far down the line.


It creates a huge incentive for both parties to commit to the sale.


#4. Due Diligence Steps
Between the offer being accepted and signing the deposit contract (or sometimes just after), you can start the due diligence checks. We always recommend hiring a legal representative to help you with these steps.


You need to apply for a Nota Simple.


The key checks from this include:
* Confirming legal ownership of the property (ensuring that you are buying from the current owner)
* Making sure there are no outstanding debts tied to the property (such as community fees or taxes)
* Verifying planning permissions and that the property is correctly and legally registered


#5. Organising Your Finances
Of course, you need to ensure your finances are properly arranged before you put in an offer on a property. But now is the time to put everything into action.


This includes:
* Transferring funds for your deposit
* Organising a mortgage (if applicable)
* Preparing for additional costs such as taxes, notary fees, and legal fees


In Spain, it’s essential to have a local bank account. You’ll not only need it to carry out the property transaction itself, but also for paying utility bills, setting up direct debits and managing ongoing property costs.

#6. Going to The Notary
Completion of the sale is always signed in front of a notary in Spain. In other countries, this key part of the process can differ, and solicitors may handle everything behind the scenes.


Both parties must attend the notary appointment, or you can assign a power of attorney if you want to.


At the notary appointment:
* The title deed (escritura) is signed
* The remaining amount is paid (a representative from the bank attends as well if you have a mortgage)
* Ownership and keys are officially transferred




Even though the transaction is performed in front of a notary, it’s still essential to get a lawyer. The notary only ensures that the transaction complies with Spanish law.


#7. After Completion
Completing your purchase is really exciting, but there are still a couple of important things you need to take care of.


* Registering the property in your name
* Transferring utilities (electricity, water, internet)
* Setting up local tax payments
* Organising ongoing property management if you’re not living there full-time


This is also when you really need to think about the practicalities of owning property abroad. You need to ensure you have the right systems in place from the beginning, which can save you a lot of time and stress later on.
How Long Does the Property Purchasing Process Take in Spain?
One of the biggest differences between buying property in Spain and in other countries is how long it takes. In Spain, the process is usually much quicker. In fact, many foreign buyers can be very surprised by this.


After having an offer accepted, it can be a matter of days before you sign the Arras Contract and transfer 10% of the price. So it can feel very daunting to make such a big commitment so quickly.


From there, it depends on whether you need to organise a mortgage or not. The seller’s circumstances, the efficiency of the documentation, legal checks and regional differences can all have an impact too.


Usually, it takes between 6 and 10 weeks from paying the deposit to completion.


Final Thoughts
Having your offer accepted on a Spanish property is a big (and exciting) step. From signing the offer contract to completing the sale at the notary, the Spanish property process can feel very different to other countries. The good news is that it’s generally efficient and you can move into your property much quicker than elsewhere.


For foreign buyers, preparation is key. You need to understand what’s ahead, get your finances in place early and work with the right professionals.
