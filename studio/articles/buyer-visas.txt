Different Spanish Visas on Offer for Foreign Property Buyers
Spain has always been a dream destination for foreigners, especially property buyers. Whether it’s a sun-soaked apartment on the Costa Blanca, a countryside finca or a vibrant city apartment in Valencia, the appeal for foreigners is obvious.


But buying property in Spain is only the first step. If you plan to spend a lot of time in the country, or even relocate there in the future, you’ll need to understand all the visa options available to you.


In this guide, we’ll outline the main Spanish visas available to non-Spanish property buyers, including requirements, benefits, and potential drawbacks. With the right visa in place, you can enjoy your home in the sun and long-term flexibility.


Do You Even Need a Visa?
In most cases, you don’t need a visa to buy property in Spain. But you might need one if you want to spend time enjoying it. If you want to spend time in Spain, it’s important to know if you need a visa to legally stay there.


EU/EEA/Swiss Citizens: You can live and work in Spain without a visa, though you’ll need to register in the country if you’re planning on staying for longer than 90 days.


Non-EU Citizens (Including UK Buyers post Brexit): You can stay in Spain for up to 90 days in any 180 days without a visa. After that, a residency visa is needed.


For many property owners, that 90-day period can feel very limited, especially if you want to spend long periods of time (like the summer months) enjoying your property.


Different Types of Visas for Foreign Property Buyers in Spain
#1. Non-Lucrative Visa (NLV)
The non-lucrative visa is one of the most popular options for property buyers who want to spend long stretches in the country without working locally. It’s a particularly popular option for retirees or individuals with sufficient financial resources.


Key Requirements
* Proof of financial means (usually around €28,000+ per year for the main applicant and an additional €600 per month per family member)
* Private health insurance in Spain
* Clean criminal record
* Medical certificate


Advantages
* Perfect for retirees or those with passive income
* You can stay for more than 90 days, which can lead to long-term residency
* The initial visa needs to be renewed after a year, and then every two years


Disadvantages
* You can’t legally work in Spain under this visa
* Financial requirements are strict
* You need to spend a lot of time in Spain to maintain your residency status


The non-lucrative visa is particularly appealing if you’ve bought property in Spain for a lifestyle move, rather than as an investment or for employment.


#2. Digital Nomad Visa
Like many other countries, Spain has introduced a digital nomad visa to attract remote workers. This visa option is really popular for property buyers who work online and meet the set requirements.


Key Requirements
* Employment with a non-Spanish company or freelance clients mostly outside of Spain
* Proof of income (around €2,500+ per month as a guideline, roughly 200% of the Spanish minimum wage)
* Relevant qualifications or work experience
* Health insurance


Advantages
* You can legally work remotely while living in Spain
* Lower income threshold compared to some other visas, which can appeal to younger workers
* Potential tax benefits under Spain’s Beckham Law
Disadvantages
* Income needs to come primarily from outside of Spain
* The application process can be difficult (there are a couple of grey areas)


If you’re a freelancer or work for a non-Spanish company and want to spend long stretches in the country whilst also having the freedom to travel and work remotely, this visa is usually perfect.


#3. Golden Visa (Now Closed to New Applicants)
The Spanish golden visa used to be a really popular way for property buyers investing €500,000 or more in real estate to automatically get a visa. But, due to recent policy changes and an attempt to regulate the property market, new applications have been discontinued.


What Does This Mean?
* New applicants can no longer apply through property investment
* If you already have a golden visa, you can still renew it – as long as you meet the original conditions
* Applications already in progress may still be processed, but this depends on the exact timing


Why Was The Golden Visa So Popular?
The main reason why Spain’s golden visa was so popular was that there was no minimum stay requirement. Receiving residency was also much quicker than other routes, and family members were included.


Property buyers in Spain who don’t already hold a golden visa and didn’t apply before the cutoff date will need to think about alternative routes.


#4. Work Visa
If you plan to move to Spain to work for a Spanish company, you’ll likely need a Spanish work visa. There are two types of work visas – an employee work visa and a self-employed (autónomo) visa.


Key Requirements
* Official job offer from a Spanish company (for employee visas)
* Viable business plan, proof of qualifications, financial stability, and relevant permits (for self-employed visas)
Advantages
* Full legal right to work in Spain
* Path to long-term residency


Disadvantages
* It can be difficult to find employers willing to sponsor an employee visa
* Long bureaucratic process
* Not a good choice for those wanting to buy property in the country purely for a lifestyle change


For most people buying property in Spain, this isn’t the ideal route. Relocation needs to be tied directly to employment or entrepreneurship, which doesn’t fit all foreign buyers.


#5. Student Visa
Student visas aren’t typically associated with property ownership, but they can be relevant in certain cases – particularly for younger buyers or families.


Key Requirements
* Proof of enrollment in a recognised educational institution
* Sufficient financial means
* Health insurance


Advantages
* Allows students to stay in Spain for extended periods
* Is sometimes converted into a work or residency permit later on


#6. Disadvantages
* Limited work rights
* It’s a temporary visa, depending on the student's status


While this visa may not be an option for many, it could be part of a longer-term strategy to move to Spain and buy property.


#6. Family Reunification Visa
If you have close family who are legally residing in Spain, this visa type lets you join them, which can then allow you to buy property and spend time in the country.


Key Requirements
* Proof of family relationship
* Sponsor (a family member in Spain) must meet the income and housing requirements for you to join them


Advantages
* Enables families to live together in Spain
* Can lead to long-term residency rights


Disadvantages
* Applicants are dependent on the other person’s residency status
* Administrative process can be lengthy and complicated


Choosing the Right Visa for Your Property Plans
Choosing the right visa ultimately depends on how you intend to use your Spanish property and whether you meet the key requirements.


If you’re just planning to occasionally visit your holiday home and, for example, rent it out the rest of the year, you can stay within the 90-day limit without needing a visa.


But, for longer stays without working, the non-lucrative visa is often the most popular choice – especially for retirees. If you do want to work and live in Spain, there are plenty of visa options that allow you to work, regardless of whether you’re self-employed, a digital nomad or employed in Spain.
