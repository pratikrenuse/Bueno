Differences Between Seasonal Lets and Tourist Rentals in Spain
For foreign property owners in Spain, understanding the distinction between seasonal lets and tourist rentals is essential. While they may appear similar on the surface, the legal frameworks, income potential and compliance requirements can differ significantly. Choosing the right rental model for you is crucial, but in order to do that, you need to understand the different structures in Spain.


In this guide, we’ll break down the key differences, main legal implications and financial considerations to help you make informed decisions about your property and rental potential in Spain.


What Is a Tourist Rental?
Imagine you’re going on a city break for a long weekend. You look up a central apartment in Madrid on Airbnb or Booking and book it for 3 nights. This is a tourist rental (often referred to as a holiday let).


This type of rental is designed for immediate occupancy, and the price per night tends to be much more expensive than long-term rentals.


Tourist rentals are characterised by:


* Short stays (usually a few days or weeks)
* Frequent tenant turnover (every couple of days)
* Fully furnished, ready-to-use properties
* Higher price per night (price may also vary throughout low and high season)


A really important difference between tourist and seasonal rentals is that tourist rentals are regulated on a regional level. Some regions, such as Barcelona, are much stricter than other parts of Spain, so you must check locally for which rules apply to you and your property.


What Is a Seasonal Let?
A seasonal let is also a temporary rental, but it lasts longer than a tourist one. The key difference, however, is the purpose of the rental. These rentals are intended for tenants who need accommodation for a specific, justified reason.


You’ll need to put this justification in your yearly NRA declaration, so it’s important you ask tenants in advance the reason why they’re coming to Spain.

Justified reasons for seasonal lets include:
* Temporary work assignments
* Study or university stays
* Medical treatment
* Relocation periods


It’s also worth mentioning that seasonal lets are very popular amongst Spaniards, especially in summer. Anyone who lives in the country will know that a lot of Spain closes for August, and many Spaniards spend the school holidays renting out accommodation at the beach. It’s common for families to rent out property for 6 weeks and enjoy the cooler coastal temperatures.


Unlike tourist rentals, seasonal lets tend to fall under Spain’s national Urban Leases Act (LAU), which governs residential rental agreements.


Key Differences Between Seasonal Lets and Tourist Rentals
#1. Purpose of Stay
As mentioned, the most fundamental difference lies in why the tenant is renting the property.


Tourist Rentals: Leisure or holiday stays
Seasonal Lets: Temporary but purposeful stays (work, health, study, etc.)


This distinction is really important because Spanish authorities increasingly look beyond contract labels. If your property is working like a holiday rental, it may be treated as one – even if you call it a seasonal let.


#2. Licensing Requirements
One of the biggest differences, and an area that foreign property owners really need to be aware of, is licensing.


Tourist Rentals:
* Require a tourism license (many regions of Spain have stopped issuing new tourist licenses, so you may not be able to rent out a newly brought properly to tourists)
* Need an NRA code if listed online (on sites such as Booking and Airbnb)
* May require approval from the building’s community of owners


Seasonal Lets:
* Do not require a tourism license
* Still require an NRA registration code if listed on a booking site online


Failing to obtain the correct license for a tourist rental can lead to heavy fines and being taken off listing platforms.


#3. Duration and Flexibility
Tourist Rentals:
* Days or a few weeks (must be shorter than a month)
* No contract needed


Seasonal Lets:
* Flexible duration (weeks to months, but less than one year)
* Requires a formal contract. There are several things you should include in the contract to minimise the possibility of landlord-tenant disputes.


#4. Taxation and Financial Implications
Both rental types require you to declare income in Spain, but there are a few differences to be aware of.


Tourist Rentals:
* May soon face higher VAT (up to 21%) or short stays
* Can charge more per night, but owners have to deal with fluctuating occupancy
* Non-resident owners need to fill out a Non-Resident Tax Return


Seasonal Lets:
* Offer more stable, predictable income streams at an overall lower price per night
* Non-residents still need to pay non-resident income tax


#5. Administrative Burden
Owners need to be well organised for both types of rentals and stay on top of many administrative tasks. Generally speaking, however, tourist rentals are more intense.


Tourist Rentals:
* Managing check-ins and check-outs (often multiple times per week)
* Cleaning schedules in between tenants
* Maintaining records
* Complying with local tourist inspections


Seasonal Lets:
* Writing up contracts for each tenant
* Complying with local rules (such as the NRA number)
* Usually involves less day-to-day management


#6. Community Restrictions
Tourist rentals can sometimes be banned or restricted by the community of owners, especially if there’s been a problem in the past. Seasonal lets, however, cannot usually be restricted in the same way.


This makes seasonal lets an attractive option in buildings where tourist licences are not permitted.


Which Option Is Right for You and Your Property?
Choosing between a seasonal let and a tourist rental depends on your priorities as a property owner.


Tourist Rentals Can Be a Good Idea If:
* You want maximum short-term income potential
* Your property is in a highly desirable tourist area, like a town on the Costa Blanca
* You can obtain the necessary licences (many regions aren’t giving out new tourist licences)
* You have the time or the structure to manage the rental property efficiently


Seasonal Lets May Suit You If:
* You prefer stable, lower-risk income
* Your building restricts tourist rentals
* You don’t want to target the holiday crowd, but rather professionals, students or those relocating
* Your property is in a popular town with Spanish tourists who are used to renting for longer periods over the summer


Final Thoughts
While seasonal lets and tourist rentals might sound the same, they are fundamentally different in legal, financial and operational terms.


For foreign property owners in Spain, understanding the key differences isn’t just helpful – it’s essential. Spain is constantly updating regulations and enforcement when it comes to renting, so choosing the right rental model from the outset can protect your investment and maximise your returns.
