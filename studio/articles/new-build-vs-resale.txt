New Build vs Resale in Spain: Which Is Right for Foreign Buyers?
Buying property in Spain is an exciting step. Whether you’re settling into retirement, planning a future move or dreaming of a sun-soaked holiday home, Spain has so much to offer foreign buyers.


But one of the first big decisions you’ll have to make is whether to buy a new build or a resale property.


Both options come with their own advantages, challenges and things to think about – especially for foreign buyers navigating a difficult legal system, unfamiliar processes and a language barrier.


In this guide, we’ll break down everything you need to consider so you can make the right choice for your taste, budget and long-term plans.


What’s The Difference Between New Build and Resale Property in Spain?
That may sound a little obvious, but it’s important to clarify the differences. New builds are brand new properties, often purchased off-plan or directly from the developer.


Resale properties are homes that have been previously owned.


Newbuilds are particularly popular in certain areas as they’re often designed with international buyers in mind. Meanwhile, resale homes tend to offer more character and are often in better locations.


New Build Vs Resale In Spain: Key Considerations
Price and Hidden Costs
At first glance, resale properties are often listed as cheaper. But that’s not always the full picture when it comes to the true cost of buying property in Spain.


New build costs:
* Typically higher purchase price (especially in popular buying areas)
* VAT (IVA) instead of transfer tax


Resale costs:
* Lower purchase price in many cases
* Property transfer tax (ITP) applies
* Potential renovations or upgrade costs


When buying property in Spain, it’s important to look beyond the initial property price. You need to plan for future expenses and weigh up whether a new build (which in theory should need fewer modifications) will actually come out cheaper in the long run.


It’s also essential to think about property insurance and ensure you get a good deal for the type of property you own.


Location and Lifestyle
Location is one of the biggest considerations when buying property (in any country). Resale homes are usually in established areas which are closer to beaches, town centres and traditional neighbourhoods.


New builds are often in expanding areas on the outskirts of town. They may also be in purpose-built developments that need more space to accommodate amenities such as swimming pools and communal areas.


If you want traditional Spanish charm and the option to walk places, resale properties may suit you better.


Design, Taste and Customisation
This is arguably the biggest difference between new builds and resale properties in Spain. New builds offer contemporary design that’s often slicker, less cluttered, and more energy efficient. If the property is still in the building process, there may be the opportunity to customise finishes, so you can really make it your own from day one.


Resale homes, however, have more traditional Spanish charm. You get immediate character, especially when it’s a well-cared for, older building that’s used good-quality materials. Of course, this does, however, mean there might be a potential need for refurbishment.


You could also go for a resale property that’s been newly renovated, and then you get the best of both worlds!


Bureaucracy and Buying Process
Spain’s buying process can feel really complex, especially if it differs from what’s normal in your country. There are lots of stages, big financial commitments and complicated paperwork, which can all be tricky to navigate. The buying process can also differ between municipalities and between new builds vs resale properties.


The buying process for new builds:
* Is often more structured with a clearer timeline (but this timeline may change depending on the building process)
* Is often done in stages. You’ll pay different amounts at different times.
* Developer-led.


The buying process for resale properties:
* Involves more negotiation (it’s common in Spain to negotiate property prices heavily)
* A faster completion timeline. Buying property in Spain often happens much quicker than foreigners expect it to.


Whether you decide to go for a new build or a resale, you’ll need
* A NIE number
* Spanish bank account
* Professional legal representation

Maintenance and Running Costs
A big difference between buying resale properties and new builds is the amount of maintenance and running costs you can expect to pay. Of course, badly built newbuilds can be very expensive long term and well looked after resale properties may not need any maintenance for years. But in general, you can expect to pay less in the early years for maintaining new builds.


It’s also important to think about community fees. Some new build developments might have higher community fees because the common areas, such as swimming pools, are more modern and are therefore more expensive.


Before buying, always check the expected community fees applicable to your property.


Rental Potential and Investment
Many foreigners buy property in Spain with the intention of renting it out when they’re not using it.


New builds:
* Are sometimes more attractive to holiday rentals (as they think they’re more reliable)
* Tend to have modern appliances, which are appealing
* Are usually bigger than traditional Spanish apartments/homes, which can accommodate families.


Resale properties:
* Are often located in prime locations such as close to the beach, town center or near to amenities.
* Are generally more unique and appeal to holiday makers who want tradition and charm.
* May require upgrading to stand a chance in the competitive Spanish holiday rental market.


If you do want to rent out your property short-term to tourists, you’ll need a tourist license and an NRA. It’s important to keep up to date with rental changes and tax obligations in Spain to ensure you’re compliant.


So, Which Is Right for You?
There’s no single right answer to whether you should buy a new or resale property in Spain. It really comes down to your priorities.


You should choose a new build if you want modern design, energy efficiency, minimal maintenance and a more straightforward buying process.


Resale properties, however, are a good choice if you want better locations, character and charm, and potentially a lower upfront cost.
