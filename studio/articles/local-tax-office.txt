Your Local Tax Office in Spain: What It Does and Why It Matters
When you buy property in Spain, understanding how local taxes work is just as important as signing the deeds or setting up your utilities. But knowing which taxes to pay can be very difficult.


Every town and city has its own Oficina de Recaudación Municipal – the local tax office responsible for managing property-related payments such as IBI, the ‘Basura’ (rubbish) tax and vehicle tax.


For many foreign property owners in Spain, this office becomes a key point of contact. You’ll receive numerous letters from them, and it’s where you’ll handle most of your ongoing property owner obligations, including annual bills, permits, and local fees.


In this guide, we’ll explain exactly what your Oficina de Recausación Municipal does and which taxes you need to pay them. We’ll also take you through opening a Bueno Spanish bank account so you can easily manage all your payments – wherever you live.


What Is Your Local Tax Office in Spain?
Understanding how bureaucracy works in a foreign country can be a bit of a headache. One of the most important pieces of the puzzle in Spain is the Oficina de Recaudación Municipal. This differs from the other tax system, which is the national tax agency (Agencia Tributaria), that handles income tax, VAT and other state-level taxes.


The Oficina de Recaudación Municipal is responsible for collecting municipal taxes and fees on behalf of your Ayuntamiento (the Town Hall). Each municipality in Spain manages its own local taxes, though in some areas, these duties are handled by a provincial collection agency – such as Suma Gestión Tributaria in Alicante or the Patronato de Recaudación in Málaga.
For foreign property owners, the Oficina de Recaudación is one of the most important local institutions to know about.


Property Tax in Spain (IBI)
The most well-known and one of the most important local taxes is the Impuesto sobre Bienes Inmuebles, or IBI. IBI is Spain’s annual property tax, which all property owners must pay. The payment is calculated based on the property’s cadastral value (which differs from its market value) and is collected by your local Oficina de Recaudación.


Waste Collection Tax (Tasa de Basura)
If you’ve bought property in Spain, even if you don’t live there full-time, you need to pay waste collection tax. This often catches foreign homeowners by surprise as it differs from other countries.


This particular tax goes towards the collection and treatment of household waste and recycling in your municipality.


It’s usually the owner of the property who has to pay the tax, although in some municipalities the occupier of the property may be charged. The amount you’ll need to pay varies by municipality and type of property. It can range from around €50 to €200 per year, depending on factors such as the size of the property, whether it’s residential or commercial, and local waste policies. Some councils calculate it based on water consumption or the number and variety of bins provided.


So, when do you pay waste collection tax and how? Most municipalities issue the payment once a year (usually between June and October), although the exact dates depend on your area and town hall. You’ll either be sent the bill via post, or it will be made available online through your Oficina de Recaudación’s payment portal.


You can pay it online or in person at a collaborating bank, but the easiest option is to set up a direct debit so you can’t forget to pay! Setting up a Bueno bank account is a great way to ensure payments happen automatically every year and for all your property banking needs.


Vehicle Tax (Impuesto sobre Vehículos de Tracción Mecánica – IVTM)
If you own a car, motorbike or any other type of roadworthy vehicle, you’ll need to pay IVTM (which is vehicle tax or otherwise known as road tax). This is another tax that’s collected by the Oficina de Recaudación Municipal because it goes directly towards funding local road maintenance and infrastructure.


The person or company listed as the owner of the vehicle is responsible for paying the annual tax. The payment dates are set by your local tax office, and they change from municipality to municipality, although it’s often due on the 1st of January.


How much road tax you’ll need to pay depends on your vehicle’s fiscal horsepower, emissions and where the car is registered. In general, smaller or low-power vehicles pay around €20-€60 per year. Mid-sized cars can pay up to €150 and powerful or high-emission vehicles can exceed €200 annually. You may have to pay higher tax rates in cities or more built-up areas. Some councils also offer discounts for electric or low-emission vehicles in a bid to meet environmental goals.


Like other taxes collected by the Oficina de Recaudación Municipal, you can pay online, at the bank or by direct debit through a Spanish bank account.
