# TASTE.md

How Bueno and 24/7 Spain sound, taken from the people who already wrote it.

This file is built from all 53 posts on getbueno.com/blog (2023 to 2025, bylined "Maria, the Bueno Team"). Those posts were written by people, and it shows: in the asides, the local detail, the slightly Nordic bluntness and even the typos. That is the voice we lost when we started generating copy. This file puts it back, minus the habits we have since outgrown.

Read it before writing anything public: blog, Facebook groups, newsletter, site pages, emails, ads. LinkedIn has its own stricter guide from John (section 8). Brand rules (no "bank" for Bueno, no competitor names, no em dashes, no emojis, no fear) sit on top of everything here.

---

## 1. Who is talking

The blog's own author line says it best: "As expats, we know Spain and have experienced the ups and downs of owning property in Spain."

That is the voice. Not a company explaining Spain. A small team of people who own property here, have stood in the queue, and are telling a friend what they learned.

- **"We" is the team, and we have been there.** "Trust us, this is a big one." "We always advise applying for an extension permit before you start work on your property." "We are often asked about the costs of owning a property in Spain."
- **"You" is one person, spoken to directly.** Never "foreigners", "buyers" or "property owners" as a crowd when we can say "you".
- **"Most of us" folds us in with the reader.** "When most of us buy property in a foreign country, it's with the idea of spending some time there." "For most of us, southern Spain is a summer holiday destination."
- **Northern European at heart.** The reader is usually Norwegian, Swedish, Danish, German, Dutch, French or British, owns a second home, often pays cash, and finds Spanish bureaucracy tiring rather than exotic. The best posts compare Spain with home: "In the UK, for example, banks switch direct debit payments automatically." Do that with Norway, Sweden and Germany too.

## 2. The moves that are ours

These are the patterns that make our posts read like us and not like everyone else. Use them on purpose.

**2.1 Ask the reader's real question, in the reader's words, then answer it flat.**
Our best headings are things a person would actually blurt out:
- "Wait, So Someone Can Just Live In My Property In Spain And There's Nothing I Can Do?" Answer: "Well, not exactly."
- "OK, So What Is A Nota Simple?"
- "So, is an accountant worth it?"
- Headings in the owner's first person: "I do not rent out the property", "I bought my property this year", "What happens if I miss the non-resident tax return deadline?"

Then the answer comes short and plain: "The short answer is no." "Bueno operates differently."

Generic copy asks the writer's question ("What is a Nota Simple?"). We ask the reader's ("OK, so what is a Nota Simple?").

**2.2 The small honest aside, in brackets.**
- "The (slightly) bad news is that you need to maintain it."
- "Unfortunately, the seller might not be telling the truth (it happens)."
- "Bank guarantees are issued at no cost to the buyer (which is great news for you)."
- "(it's way more than adopting a siesta every afternoon)"
- "#3. Use Professionals (Even If You're a DIY Whizz)"
- "Step 5: Plan ahead (so it doesn't happen again!)"

One per piece, two at most. It is where the personality lives.

**2.3 Lived detail that only someone who has been there would know.**
- "Cala Blanca is a wonderful, hidden beach in Jávea, accessible on foot through a little hole in the cliff."
- "There aren't any facilities on the beach, so make sure you bring plenty of water and something to eat."
- "Roofs are so often overlooked in Spanish properties."
- "Sea air, although beautiful to breathe, can be a nightmare for properties."
- "Not all staff in the police stations will speak English, so it's worth jotting down the main words."
- "Get there early to beat the queues."
- "You won't be able to choose your water provider though."

A competitor can copy a fact. They cannot copy "a little hole in the cliff". Every piece needs at least one detail like this, and it must be true.

**2.4 Numbers from our own experience.**
- "based on experiences from our customers, we have created a simple overview"
- "60-70% of foreigners who buy property in Spain do not take out loans from Spanish banks."
- "NIE can be as quick as issued the same day but for most it takes 1-2 weeks."

Real ranges, from real work. Only use a number we can stand behind; if it comes from our own customers, say so.

**2.5 Blunt, practical sentences.**
The operations posts read like a Norwegian colleague talking, and they are some of our best:
- "Buying rural land in Spain is a daunting process."
- "You must check all the information you get from the seller."
- "Registering the property is the responsibility of the buyer."
- "Gas is less popular in Spain than in many other European countries."
- "Only emergency dental treatment is included in the national healthcare system in Spain."

Say the thing. No warm-up.

**2.6 Honest about Spain, fond of it anyway.**
- "Buying a house is difficult enough in your own country, let alone in a foreign one."
- "The different forms, dates and obligations can be a big challenge, and that's before language gets involved."
- "Life here is slower, queues are longer, bureaucracy is more frustrating, and people are louder."
- "'Ahora' Isn't Always Now."
- "Of course, there are some differences which might take a bit of getting used to, but that's part of the charm."

We never complain about Spain and never pretend it is easy. Both at once is our tone.

**2.7 Reassure, do not alarm.**
- "Don't panic, there are still a couple of things you can do."
- "Well, not exactly."
- "By taking the right precautionary measures, you'll be able to enjoy your Spanish property worry-free."

**2.8 End on enjoying the home.**
Our closings send people back to their life, not to a form: "so you can concentrate on enjoying your home in Spain", "more time enjoying the beautiful country", "concentrate on enjoying spending time with them in Spain". Keep this. It is the "relief" in Hook, Pain, Solution, Relief, CTA.

**2.9 Spanish terms, glossed once.**
English first, Spanish in brackets, or the reverse with a plain gloss: "community fees (gastos de comunidad)", "city councils (ayuntamientos)", "IBI, the local property tax". When a word is fun, explain it: "Plus means 'more' or 'extra' and valía means 'value'." After the first use, just use it.

**2.10 British spelling.** whilst, favourite, licence (noun), personalised, organise, metre. Pick it and keep it. Euro sign before the number: €99, €4,000.

## 3. What we keep from the old blog, and what we drop

The old posts are the voice, not the rulebook. Some habits came from SEO templates and older brand rules.

**Keep:** the you/we expat voice, reader-voice questions, bracket asides, lived detail, customer-based numbers, blunt practical sentences, fond honesty about Spain, the "enjoy your home" close, British spelling.

**Drop:**
- "bank", "banking", "property banking", "Bueno bank account", "banking alternative". It is the Bueno Account, and Bueno is a finance platform.
- Naming other providers. Several old posts name Spanish lenders and their fees. We no longer do that.
- Fines as the hook ("fines range between €4,000 and €60,000"). State a consequence once, calmly, if it matters.
- Adjective soup: amazing, stunning, wonderful, incredible, fabulous, idyllic, crystal clear, dream home, beloved home. One honest adjective beats four.
- Template scaffolding: "Everything You Need to Know" titles, "In this article, we're going to delve into", "Final Thoughts", "Conclusion", "There are so many reasons why".
- The "Spain is amazing. However, ..." opener. Everyone writes it.
- Copy-paste closers ("Get in touch with the team at Bueno to..."). Write a closing that fits the piece.
- More than one exclamation mark per piece.
- Product-page voice ("ensuring the safety of your finances is paramount", "redefines peace of mind"). This is the one old post that sounds machine-made. Never again.
- Dashes as pauses. Use a full stop or a comma.

## 4. The AI tells (never)

On top of section 3, these mark copy as generated. Remove every one.

- "Here's the thing", "Here's what you need to know", "Let's break it down", "Let's dive in", "delve"
- "Nobody tells you", "What most people don't realise", "The part that catches people out"
- "Navigating", "the maze of", "landscape", "journey"
- "Whether you're X or Y" as an opener
- "It's worth noting", "Importantly", "Crucially", "Simply put", "The good news is", "The bottom line"
- "seamless", "hassle-free", "quietly", "genuinely", "unlock", "empower", "elevate", "streamline", "game-changer"
- "It isn't X. It's Y." openers
- Generic question openers ("Planning to buy in Spain?"). A real reader's question, in their words, is fine (2.1). A marketing question is not.
- Reflex lists of exactly three.
- Every paragraph the same length.
- A closing line that repeats the opening.
- Label-colon lines ("Fee: €9.84."). Write the sentence.

## 5. What only we have (use at least one in every piece)

1. **The real question.** From Crisp, Maria's inbox, Facebook comments or sales calls. Anonymised, first name or city only. Never invented.
2. **The home-country angle.** What the Norwegian, Swedish, Danish, German, Dutch or French tax office wants alongside the Spanish rule.
3. **What we see in our own work.** Patterns from filing Modelo 210 and running accounts, stated as observation, or as a number only when Pratik has supplied it.
4. **The specific place.** The town, the office, the season, the little hole in the cliff.
5. **The team.** An approved line from Felix, Maria or John.

## 6. One example: the NIE

**Generic (what everyone publishes):**
> Planning to buy property in Spain? Here's what you need to know about the NIE. The NIE (Número de Identidad de Extranjero) is a crucial identification number for foreigners. Whether you're buying a home or opening an account, you'll need one. The good news is the process is straightforward.

**Ours:**
> You'll need an NIE (número de identidad de extranjero) for almost everything here: buying, selling, paying tax, even setting up the electricity. It's the number Spain gives you as a foreigner, and once you have it, it's yours for life.
>
> So what if you got one years ago and can't find the paper? You don't need a new number (we get asked this a lot). You can request a new certificate for the same one with form EX-15 and the fee of €9.84. Before you do, look through old Spanish paperwork: deeds, tax letters, a utility contract. Your NIE starts with an X, Y or Z.
>
> Book an appointment if your office offers them. If not, get there early. Not everyone at the counter will speak English, so it's worth writing down the words you need.
>
> Then go and enjoy the place you got the number for.

What makes it ours: the reader's real question, a bracket aside, lived detail from the old post ("get there early", "writing down the words you need"), a calm close about the home. Use "we get asked this a lot" only when it is true.

## 7. Per channel

**Blog and site pages.** The full voice from section 2. A reader-voice question in at least one heading. One bracket aside. One lived detail. Bueno appears where it really helps, usually once near the end, never as a product page in disguise. Many of our best posts did not mention Bueno in the body at all; that is fine for 24/7 Spain.

**Facebook groups.** The voice of a member who has been through it. Shorter. Open with the reader's question or the plain answer, never with a sales line. Sponsor line on 24/7 Spain posts.

**Newsletter.** News items: short heading, two or three sentences. Trends: five words or fewer, exactly two sentences. Each item says what it means for an owner, in the same warm, blunt voice.

**Ads.** Hook, Pain, Solution, Relief, CTA. The hook is a lived moment (a letter in Spanish on the doormat, a bill that doubled), and the relief is the enjoy-your-home close.

**LinkedIn.** John's guide wins here and is stricter: plain subject opener, no "we", no anecdotes, no rhetorical questions, Spanish legal term in brackets, 250 to 600 words, fixed closing line. Borrow only 2.3 (lived detail, without "we"), 2.5 (blunt sentences) and 2.9 (Spanish terms) from this file.

---

## 8. Before publishing: the six checks

1. **Swap test.** Swap our name for a competitor's. If it still works, add something from section 5.
2. **Voice test.** Is there at least one reader-voice question, bracket aside or lived detail (except on LinkedIn)?
3. **Opening test.** Does the first line say something only this piece says? "Spain is amazing" fails.
4. **Tells test.** Nothing from sections 3 and 4.
5. **Fact test.** Every number, form, fee and deadline is verified against an official source or our fact bases.
6. **Read-aloud test.** Would Maria say this to a customer on the phone? If not, rewrite it.
