NEWS: This fortnight in Spanish property

1. Used home prices hit an all-time record
Used homes in Spain averaged 2,955 euros per square metre in October, 11.2% more than a year ago and the highest idealista has ever recorded. Málaga, Palma, Barcelona and Madrid are all above 3,000 euros, and Madrid and Barcelona are now above 5,000. It is a good moment to see what your own home is worth today.

2. 26,531 rental listings vanished in one month
After the government's first housing decrees on 29 September, rental listings on idealista fell by 19% in a single month. Two new decrees have taken their place, and Congress votes on them on Thursday 12 November. If you let your home, that vote decides which rules you will be letting under.

3. The Supreme Court strikes down a 50 cent a day late fee
One community of owners charged 50 cents for every day a fee was late, which could reach 182.50 euros a year on a garage fee of 135 euros. Spain's Supreme Court called it disproportionate and struck it down. Communities can still chase unpaid fees, but their penalties have to stay reasonable.

REGION SPOTLIGHT: Marbella and the western Costa del Sol
The OECD has named the Málaga and Marbella area as the hardest place in Spain to buy a home. Buying here takes 4.52 more years of income than the Spanish average, ahead of Palma de Mallorca at 4.33 years.
Prices tell the same story. Used homes in Málaga city are now above 3,000 euros per square metre, according to idealista's October figures. Across Spain, fewer homes are being sold than at any time in six years, yet the average price of those that do sell is up 12.8% on last year.

READER'S QUESTION: "Our community wants to fine owners who pay their fees late. Are they allowed to do that?"
Yes, a community of owners can act against owners who do not pay, and it can agree on penalties at a meeting. But the Supreme Court has just made it clear that those penalties must be in proportion to what is owed. A flat charge that keeps growing every day, whatever the amount, can be struck down.
If you disagree with a decision your community has taken, you can challenge it in court. The usual deadline is three months from the meeting, or one year if the decision goes against the law.
The simplest protection is never to be late. Most owners already pay their community fees by direct debit, so check that yours comes from an account that always has money in it.
